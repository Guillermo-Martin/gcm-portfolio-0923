Designing and building a premium post-operative services landing page

Helping a nurse duo launch their personalized recovery services though branding and UX design

Overview

  • Client: Two nurses from Berkeley, CA
  • Role: UX designer and front-end web developer
  • Duration: 4 weeks

A nurse and one of her colleagues wanted to start their own business providing personalized post-operative services. They wanted to advertise their services and make it easy for patients to contact them via a referral.

Problem

Because they were in the early stages of their business, there was no established branding or online presence.

Goal

Establish a brand that would cater to high-end clientele and create a webpage that advertises their services and makes it easy to contact them.

Understanding the user

To better understand the user, I interviewed people who have had some form of surgery in the past. The goal of these interviews was to find out what type of information would be useful to see for someone wanting post-operative care.

Things to consider

  • Services provided: A majority of interviewees wanted to see what types of services would be provided.
  • Digestible information: Having information nicely organized and nicely separated so it's easy on the eyes, and so visitors aren't overloaded with information.
  • A way to connect: Because this would be patients' first look at post-operative services, a way to contact the nurses to learn more is important. This could either be through contact information or through a form.
  • Pricing: A majority of interviewees want to see pricing information, payment options, and what types of insurance is accepted.

Persona

From the information I gathered from the interviewees and considering the business' target audience, I created a persona to help inform my design decisions.

Hi, I'm Lisa Archibald.

"I want recovery after my surgery to be as smooth as possible."

Lisa, a real estate agent in Beverly Hills, recently had surgery and is overwhelmed by the recovery process. She wants her recovery to be smooth, but is frustrated by how difficult it is to find information on what post-op care actually involves. She wants to make the best decision for her health, but has little knowledge on the services and on whether or not she can afford it. She’d like an easy way to see and learn about her options, the benefits of such care, and connect with someone to determine what’s best for her.

Goals
  • Sign up for post-op care
  • Be informed enough to make the decision to sign up for post-op care
  • Contact someone for a consultation
Frustrations
  • Have no idea what post-op care entails
  • Have trouble finding out information on the type of care they need
  • Seeing if they can afford it

Starting the design

Inspiration

Since I was starting from scratch, I conducted a competitive audit and looked at a few websites. One of the goals was to determine what type of information would be helpful to user and see how information was laid out. I also took screenshots of different "high-class" looking websites to help inform my design direction.

Wireframes

With some inspiration in mind, I sketched out different sections to include and potential layouts. I indicated which layouts I liked the best, and combined them all into a single wireframe.

Digital wireframes

Using that final wireframe sketch, I created a low-fidelity digital wireframe. I used this to easily show the client what the page would look like and to get any feedback on the layout.
