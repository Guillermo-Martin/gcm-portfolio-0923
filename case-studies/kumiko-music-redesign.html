Kumiko Music: Creating an easy to navigate and informative user experience

Showcasing a local musician's work and accomplishments for the world to enjoy

Photo by Geert Pieters on Unsplash

Overview

  • Client: Kumiko Uyeda, pianist and composer
  • Role: UX Designer
  • Duration: 4 weeks

Kumiko Uyeda, a local pianist, composer, and ethnolmusicologist, wanted her site to represent all faces of her music, from performing solo and chamber music, to her compositions. She wants to share her work in hope of getting more exposure.

Problem

The original site had an inconsistent design and navigational challenges, which made finding information difficult for users.

Goal

Redesign the site so pages are more cohesive, make navigation between pages easier, and layout information so visitors can learn more about Kumiko and her works.

Understanding the user

To begin, I conducted an audit of Kumiko’s current website, listing my observations, pain points, and improvement opportunities for each page of the site. Using the existing site, I conducted a usability study with several users to get more information. Using an affinity diagram, I identified some common pain points.

Pain points

  • Navigation: Users were confused by the navigation. Adding in a navbar can make it easier for users to navigate around the site and make it easy to find information.
  • Design: The designs amongst pages weren't cohesive. Creating a consistent design will make the site feel cohesive.
  • Layouts: Information was inconsistently laid out. Laying out elements and information can make it easier to digest.

When it comes to a website, you want a unifying thread.

- Participant C

Personas

To help inform my design solutions, I created two personas, both with a problem that needs solving.

Hi, I'm Shawn Watts!

"I want to learn more about this new musician, but the website made it difficult to do so."

Shawn is a photographer and during one of his gigs learned about a new musician. Wanting to learn more, he visited the musician’s website. Upon visiting the site, he became confused by the navigation and inconsistent design and layouts. This made it difficult for Shawn to understand who the musician really is and find basic information. Shawn would like a site that’s easy to navigate while also showcasing who the musician is.

Goals
  • Wants to learn more about a new musician.
Frustrations
  • Navigating the musician's website is challenging.
  • Can't tell who a musician is by their website.

Hi, I'm Louella Washington!

"I’d like to listen more of this musician’s music, but I’m having a hard time finding where."

Louella is a social worker and likes to relax by exploring new music. She recently came across a musician she’s interested in and wants to listen to more of their music, as well as watch some of their performances. When she visits the musician’s site, she’s a bit lost; she can’t easily find where to play music, and when she clicks on links they take her to YouTube, instead of playing on the musician’s site. Louella would like to easily find samples and watch performances on the musician’s site.

Goals
  • Listen to music by a new musician.
  • Watch the musician perform.
Frustrations
  • Unsure where to listen and play samples of music.
  • Links take her off the musician’s site to another site.

Starting the design

Wireframes

I created wireframes for five different pages. One of my goals was to make it easy for a user to find different types of information and content. So I created wireframes for five different pages: Home, About, Compositions, Pianist, and Contact. I experimented with different layouts to determine the best way to organize different types of information.

Digital wireframes

I used a combination of different font sizes and weights, as well as whitespace to provide hierarchy and organization for different sections on the page.

High-fidelity mockups

What I focused on

  • Navigation: Making it easy to move between pages.
  • Design and layout: Consistency throughout the site and making sure Kumiko's personality and accomplishments shine through.
  • Readability: Ensuring visitors aren't overwhelemed by information.

Navigation

The previous design had a single page that served as the main point for navigation. Other pages didn't have a way to navigate around. The new designs incorporate a navbar at the top of every page allowing users to easily navigate the site.

Design and layout

Each page had a different layout. Elements weren’t laid out uniformly. Furthermore, each page looked different; users thought that because of this, there was a lack of cohesiveness throughout the site. I laid out elements in a similar fashion for each page and used consistent font sizes. I used section headers followed by their main content, as well as white space to further designate sections on a page. I also wanted to highlight Kumiko's work and make sure visitors can easily understand who she is.

Readability

Users found the Ethnomusicology content to be very text heavy and very hard to read. I used headers and white space to make it easier to read.

Prototype

Using the mockups, I created a high-fidelity prototype. I tested this prototype with different users to determine if the new designs fixed problems associated with the old site.

Try the prototype!

Usability testing

Using the high-fidelity prototype, I conducted a usability study to see if the new designs tackled the pain points users faced with the site’s previous design.

  • Type: Moderated usability study
  • Location: United States, remote
  • Participants: 3 participants
  • Length: 30-45 minutes

Findings

  • Improved navigation: Participants didn’t have any trouble navigating the site and found it straightforward. Though, some thought the navbar links might be confusing.
  • Personal touch: Participants liked the “personal touch” the site had and wanted to learn more about what inspires her.
  • Consistency: Participants liked how the information was structured and organized. Although, the Ethnomusicology page was still text heavy.

I feel like instead of calling it "Pianist" it could be called something like "Music".

- Participant B
